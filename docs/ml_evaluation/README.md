## Protocol

- **Temporal split, as in `ml/train_points_v4.py`:** train 2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24; early stopping on 2024-25 (best iteration 105 trees); test 2025-26 and 2026-27. Hyperparameters are `ml.points_model.XGB_PARAMS`, chosen on the validation season. Ablations and seed repeats use the same split. The last-match and 20-match team-strength features were added in October 2026 after a rolling-origin backtest (`scripts/analysis/ml_backtest.py`) whose folds include the test seasons; they also win on the 2023-24 and 2024-25 folds alone, but the test numbers are no longer fully untouched.
- **No-leakage design:** one row per player per fixture; player features use only earlier gameweeks (a double gameweek's two fixtures share the pre-deadline values); team strength comes from a separate team-per-match table, also rolled over earlier gameweeks; FPL's historical xP is not a feature (it was recorded inside the gameweek).
- **Metrics:** regression metrics per row (player x fixture). Pick metrics per gameweek after summing a player's double-gameweek fixtures: top-k = mean actual points of the k highest predictions; captain = top-1; hit@20 = share of our top 20 who scored at least the gameweek's 20th-best score; oracle = ranking by actual points. Gameweek values are averaged with equal weight.
- **Baselines on identical rows:** zero (equivalent to a random pick for ranking), training-set position mean, last-3 and last-6 average points, and season-to-date points per game (falling back to last-6, then 0, before a player's first match of the season). Missing histories fall back to 0, so every method is scored on the same 34,977 rows. (The training report drops rows a baseline cannot score, so its baseline numbers differ slightly.)
- **Uncertainty:** 2000 bootstrap resamples of gameweeks, stratified by season, fixed seed 42. Paired differences resample the same gameweeks for both methods.

## Leakage audit

1. **One feature row per input row.** `build_features` returned 168,302 rows for 168,302 input rows (identical); every input row id appears exactly once (0 duplicated ids).
2. **No look-ahead (perturbation test).** 2025-26 GW>=20: 22 outcome columns shuffled (15,572 rows), then rebuilt the features. Rows up to and including that gameweek changed: **0 of 148,300** (0 of 790 at the gameweek itself). Power check: 795 of 795 rows of the following gameweek changed, as they should.
3. **No suspiciously strong single feature.** Highest |Pearson r| with the target on the test seasons: `minutes_r1` 0.536, `minutes_r3` 0.535, `season_minutes_share` 0.523, `played_r3` 0.516, `minutes_r6` 0.515 (training seasons: `minutes_r3` 0.490). For contrast, FPL's xP correlates 0.69 (2023-24), 0.66 (2024-25), 0.35 (2025-26).
4. **Rank by a single column.** This is the test that exposed the xP leak: a pre-deadline signal should not pick much better than the best honest feature.

v4 is shown only where it is out of sample (2024-25 is the early-stopping season, so mildly optimistic). In the seasons where xP ranks far above every honest feature and above v4, it carries same-gameweek information (team news/lineups), so it cannot be a feature or a fair baseline.

![Single-feature ranking](fig_leakage_single_feature.png)

5. **Validation vs test consistency.** Train (in-sample) MAE 0.963 / top-20 6.86; validation 0.978 / 5.19; test 0.996 / 4.68. Row-level error is nearly the same in and out of sample, so the model is not memorising rows. Pick quality is higher in-sample, as expected for trees that have seen those hauls. The validation top-20 is above the test seasons' even though 2024-25's oracle is lower (10.86 vs 11.18): part of that gap is the mild optimism of early-stopping on that season, part is season-to-season variation. There is no sign of the near-perfect in-sample fit that leakage produces.
6. **Why the legacy v2 numbers (R² 0.72-0.87) were inflated.** Running the old `FeatureEngineer` on 2022-23 turns 26,505 rows into 721,933 (**27.2x**): its opponent-strength merges join per-player values on (season, team, GW). The `shift(-1)` "next match" target then mostly lands on a copy of the same match: for rows scoring 5+ points the target equals the row's own points 97% of the time, against 6% for a player's genuine next match. Together with context features describing the previous match and the leaky xP, that explains the R² far above the 0.55 that even same-match minutes and xG allow.

## Error analysis

- **Availability is the largest error source.** 0-minute rows are 60% of the test set and 21% of total absolute error. Players who averaged 60+ minutes over their last three matches but then didn't play (866 rows, 2.5%) were predicted 2.22 points on average and account for 6% of the absolute error. These are injuries, suspensions and rotation that the historical data can't see; the live pipeline applies FPL's availability flags after the model, which this offline evaluation does not.
- **Hauls (10+ points in a gameweek)** are 645 player-gameweeks (1.9%). 19% were in v4's top 20 and 38% in its top 50 (season pts/game: 15% / 31%); the median haul came from a player ranked in the top 10% of that gameweek. By position: GK 38 hauls (6% of hauls, 4% of v4 top-20 picks, 26% caught in top 50); DEF 207 hauls (32% of hauls, 13% of v4 top-20 picks, 24% caught in top 50); MID 316 hauls (49% of hauls, 61% of v4 top-20 picks, 43% caught in top 50); FWD 84 hauls (13% of hauls, 23% of v4 top-20 picks, 56% caught in top 50).
- **Little history:** 0 (debut) prior matches: n=375, predicted 0.87 vs actual 0.99, MAE 1.06; 1-9 prior matches: n=2,943, predicted 0.86 vs actual 0.88, MAE 0.76; 10-37 prior matches: n=6,631, predicted 0.82 vs actual 0.85, MAE 0.77; 38+ prior matches: n=25,028, predicted 1.26 vs actual 1.33, MAE 1.08.
- **Promoted teams** (2025-26: Burnley, Leeds, Sunderland; 2026-27: Coventry City, Hull City, Ipswich): bias -0.131 vs -0.042 for established teams, MAE 0.832 vs 1.028.
- **Rotation risk** (average minutes over the last three matches): no history: played 37%, predicted 0.87 vs actual 0.99; 0: played 5%, predicted 0.16 vs actual 0.09; 1-30: played 53%, predicted 1.07 vs actual 1.17; 31-60: played 75%, predicted 1.99 vs actual 2.13; 61-90: played 91%, predicted 2.97 vs actual 3.24.

## Limitations and future work

- **Thin availability data.** FPL's status before each deadline comes from snapshots of vaastav's players_raw.csv history (about 35 of 38 gameweeks a season to 2024-25, a dozen since) and our own archive from GW9 2026-27; without a snapshot the availability features are missing, and the model predicts an expected value over "plays" and "doesn't play".
- **Player identity is the FPL code.** In the test seasons 10 (fixture, player) pairs have two rows (Ben Gannon-Doak, Junior Kroupi), and 0 player-gameweeks mix two teams; such players' form features blend two records. Players are keyed by their FPL code, so a renamed player keeps his history.
- **Small current-season sample.** 2026-27 has 8 gameweeks, so its intervals are wide and early-season effects dominate it.
- **xG features exist only from 2023-24.** Earlier training seasons have them missing, so the model learns xG-based form from one training season.
- **Irreducible noise.** The oracle top-20 is 11.18 against v4's 4.68, but the oracle uses outcomes; a model told the match's own minutes, starts and xG/xA/xGC still only reaches R² 0.55 (MAE 0.669), and knowing each player-season's true average explains about 0.32 of the per-fixture variance. Goals, assists and bonus are rare and lumpy, so large single-match errors are expected.
- **Single model, squared-error objective.** A Tweedie/zero-inflated objective or a two-stage minutes x points model could fit the zero-inflated target better; ranking objectives (pairwise) could target pick quality directly.

## Production model check

The served `points_v4.json` (166 trees, refit on 2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26, 2026-27) scored on the same test rows: MAE 0.915, R² 0.443, Spearman 0.753, captain 10.17, top-20 6.98. **This is optimistic**: the production model was trained on these seasons. It only confirms that the deployed file loads and behaves like the evaluated one; the honest numbers are the ones above.

Cross-check: `ml.points_model.evaluate` on this run's v4 predictions gives top-20 4.682 / MAE 0.996 (combined), versus 4.682 / 0.996 in `ml/models/points_v4_report.json`.
